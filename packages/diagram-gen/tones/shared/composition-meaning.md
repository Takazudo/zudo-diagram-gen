# Composition teaching example

Every tone redraws one explanation: **pattern background + text layer → composition → image export**. Compare treatments of the same facts rather than treating each tone as a different feature. The bundled SVGs are original conceptual drawings, not screenshots or a specification of any private product.

## Read the objects and labels

- **Pattern** is an invented repeating background motif, such as stripes, dots or tiles. It supplies the image's visual ground; its sample colors are not permanent brand tokens.
- **Text** is a separate lettering layer placed above the background. **Aa**, **MAKE**, **MAKE SOMETHING**, and **Make something.** are illustrative lettering samples. They are not buttons, commands, required user input or different product features.
- **Composition** means the visible combination of background and lettering in one canvas. The destination panel contains both; it is not a third source layer.
- **PNG** means Portable Network Graphics, the example's raster image export label. This teaching drawing does not promise that an actual product supports only PNG, or document export settings. The references themselves are SVG (Scalable Vector Graphics), editable vector drawings of that story; SVG is not the depicted product output.
- **Connectors** indicate contribution and reading direction. Two inputs contribute to one combined result. Their shape, direction bends and visual weight vary by tone; they are not timing, network requests or implementation wiring.
- A **checkerboard**, when present behind lettering, indicates transparent regions in the text preview so the pattern can remain visible underneath. It is an explanatory convention, not an additional layer to export.
- **01**, **02**, and **03** in the Swiss grid example order the explanatory panels. Print offsets, shadows, grids, sketch marks and perspective are stylistic devices, not extra workflow steps.

## What must survive a new drawing

Keep the distinction between patterned ground and lettering, their combination into one image, and the export destination. Labels must remain readable at the intended placement. A palette, motif, sample slogan, connector route or perspective can change when the project's actual facts and tone permit it. Read the tone's own recipe for its geometry and construction intent.

## Omitted behavior

Optional image layers, saving/reopening, text editing controls, layer ordering controls, generator parameters, undo, authentication and exact export controls are outside this small teaching example. Their omission does not imply that a real product lacks them. No private manual is needed to interpret these drawings, and this invented example does not fully specify the private product that motivated the original study. Verify current implementation and authorized project facts before producing a product help diagram.
